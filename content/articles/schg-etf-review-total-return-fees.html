What SCHG is and who runs it

SCHG is run by Schwab Asset Management, the fund arm of Charles Schwab. It is an index fund, which means there is no manager picking stocks. The fund simply follows a Dow Jones index of large U.S. companies that screen as growth stocks.

Schwab built its ETF lineup around one idea: make the fees as close to zero as possible. SCHG is a good example. It pays out on a {{live:SCHG:freq:text}} schedule, and it carries no price decay flag in our data ({{live:SCHG:decay:decay}}), which is what you would expect from a plain index fund with no options or leverage.

What SCHG holds

The fund is a large-cap growth portfolio. It leans toward companies with faster earnings and revenue growth and higher valuations, and it skips most of the slower, cheaper parts of the market like banks, utilities and old-line industrials.

In practice, that means a big tilt toward technology and communication services, with a good helping of consumer discretionary and health care. The biggest positions are the names you already know: the large U.S. tech platforms, the major chipmakers, the e-commerce giants and the big social media and search companies. The exact weights move around every quarter, so check Schwab's site for the current list.

Here's the thing about concentration. Growth indexes are usually weighted by company size, so the largest companies get the biggest slice. A small group of mega-caps can make up a large share of the whole fund. You may own a few hundred stocks on paper, but the top handful does most of the work. That is not a flaw, but you should know it going in.

The main risks

  • Concentration. A few giant companies drive a large share of results. If they stumble, the whole fund feels it.
  • Valuation risk. Growth stocks are priced for strong future earnings. When rates rise or growth slows, prices can fall fast.
  • Overlap. If you also own VOO, QQQ or a total market fund, you already hold many of the same top names. Adding SCHG can double up your tech exposure without you noticing.
  • Recency bias. The great track record came in a period when large growth beat almost everything. That may or may not repeat.

Also, do not confuse this with leveraged funds. SCHG is a plain index fund. A fund like TQQQ is a different animal, and I explain why in leveraged ETF decay.

Who SCHG tends to fit, and who might skip it

SCHG tends to fit long-term investors who want a cheap, simple way to tilt toward large U.S. growth and who will not panic when the fund drops hard. It also works for people who already own a broad index fund and want a deliberate growth sleeve on top.

It tends to fit poorly for anyone who needs income now, since the yield is {{live:SCHG:yield}}. It is also a tough fit if you cannot stomach big drawdowns, or if you already hold a lot of tech through other funds. For those readers, a broad fund like VOO or an income-focused fund may match the goal better.
