Alpha Picks Review 2026: How Seeking Alpha's Stock Picks Work, the Track Record and Who It's For

An honest Alpha Picks review: how Seeking Alpha picks two stocks a month, the buy and sell rules, the reported track record vs the S&P 500, the price, and who should skip it.

BSBy Benjie Siegel, Founder, TopETFs
October 15, 2026·10 min read·Live data Share on X

The short version

  • Alpha Picks is a Seeking Alpha subscription that adds two stock picks a month, chosen by a rules-based system from stocks rated Quant Strong Buy. It is separate from Seeking Alpha Premium.
  • Seeking Alpha reports the model portfolio has beaten the S&P 500 by a wide margin since it launched in July 2022. That is a real track record, but it is a hypothetical portfolio and past results do not repeat on command.
  • It is built for growth, not income. If you want dividends, Seeking Alpha Premium is the better fit, and the core index funds below are still the benchmark to beat.

Disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. If you subscribe to Seeking Alpha through them, TopETFs may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Seeking Alpha does not review or approve what we publish.

If you are asking "is Alpha Picks worth it," here is my honest answer: it can be, for a specific kind of investor. Alpha Picks is a stock-picking service from Seeking Alpha that hands you two new stock ideas a month, with clear rules for when to buy and when to sell. It is not an ETF and it is not an income strategy. It is a done-for-you growth stock portfolio, and its price tag ($499 a year list price as of October 2026) means it has to beat a simple index fund like VOO by enough to earn its keep.

Let's walk through how it works, what the track record actually says, and who I think should and shouldn't sign up.

How Alpha Picks works

Alpha Picks runs on Seeking Alpha's Quant Ratings, the same system that grades thousands of stocks on value, growth, profitability, momentum and earnings revisions. Based on Seeking Alpha's own description of the service, the process looks like this:

  • Two new picks a month, released around the 1st and the 15th.
  • Only Quant Strong Buys qualify, and a stock needs to have held that rating for a long stretch (Seeking Alpha has described it as 75 or more consecutive days).
  • Only US common stocks, with a market cap above $500 million and a share price above $10. No ADRs and no REITs.
  • No repeats within a year. A stock picked in the last year isn't picked again.
  • A full portfolio of roughly 37 to 45 stocks, with a few trades a month and an intended holding period of at least a year.

The style is often described as "growth at a reasonable price." In plain English: companies with strong earnings momentum that the quant system doesn't consider wildly overvalued.

When it sells

The sell rules are just as important, and they are mechanical rather than emotional. Seeking Alpha has said a position is sold when its Quant Rating drops to Sell or Strong Sell, when it sits at Hold for an extended period (180 days, with exceptions for big winners), or when the company becomes an acquisition target. Positions that grow past 15% of the portfolio get trimmed back to 10%.

That last rule matters more than it sounds. Concentration is how a model portfolio produces huge numbers, and it is also how one bad quarter in one stock can hurt.

The track record, honestly

Seeking Alpha publishes Alpha Picks performance against the S&P 500. In a May 2026 webinar, it reported the model portfolio was up about 368% since its July 1, 2022 launch, versus roughly 96% for the S&P 500 over the same period. Those are Seeking Alpha's figures, not ours, and you can read their breakdown here.

Here's how I read that:

  • It's a strong start. Beating the index by that much over roughly four years is not nothing, and the rules were published up front.
  • It's a hypothetical portfolio. Your results depend on when you join, whether you buy every pick, and what prices you actually get.
  • A few big winners did a lot of the work. That's normal for concentrated growth portfolios, but it means the next four years could look very different.
  • The period was kind to growth. Since mid 2022, momentum and large growth companies have had a great run. For context, compare that with QQQ at 1,573% and VOO at 836% total return since their own launches in our database.

Past performance does not guarantee future results. That line is on every disclaimer for a reason.

What it costs, and what's included

As of October 2026, Alpha Picks listed at $499 a year, with first-year discounts often available. Seeking Alpha's help center says there is no free trial for Alpha Picks, though new members get access to the past picks right away. Always confirm the current price and terms on the offer page before you subscribe.

Important: Alpha Picks does not include Seeking Alpha Premium. They are separate products. Seeking Alpha does sell a bundle of the two, which makes sense if you want the picks plus the research tools to dig into them.

Who Alpha Picks is for, and who should skip it

It can make sense if you want individual growth stocks in part of your portfolio, you don't have time to screen thousands of companies, and you can stick to a rules-based system through rough patches without panic-selling.

I'd skip it if you are an income investor. Seeking Alpha itself says Alpha Picks isn't designed to generate dividend income, and many of the picks pay little or nothing. I'd also skip it if your portfolio is small enough that $499 a year is a big chunk of your expected gains. On a $10,000 portfolio, that fee is about 5% a year before you've made a dollar, while VOO costs 0.03%.

A sensible middle ground a lot of investors use: keep a core of low-cost index ETFs, and use a service like Alpha Picks only for a satellite slice of the portfolio you're comfortable being more aggressive with.

FAQ

Is Alpha Picks worth it?

It can be for growth-focused investors who want a rules-based stock portfolio and will follow it consistently. Seeking Alpha reports strong results since July 2022, but it's a hypothetical portfolio, it costs $499 a year at list price, and it isn't built for dividend income.

How much does Alpha Picks cost?

The list price was $499 per year as of October 2026. Seeking Alpha frequently runs first-year discounts, and there's also a bundle with Seeking Alpha Premium. Check the current offer before subscribing.

Does Alpha Picks have a free trial?

Seeking Alpha's help center says Alpha Picks does not offer a free trial. New subscribers get immediate access to past picks.

How many stocks does Alpha Picks recommend?

Two new picks a month, usually around the 1st and the 15th. The full model portfolio typically holds roughly 37 to 45 stocks.

Is Alpha Picks included with Seeking Alpha Premium?

No. Alpha Picks and Premium are separate subscriptions. Seeking Alpha sells a bundle if you want both.

Is Alpha Picks good for dividend investors?

Not really. Seeking Alpha says Alpha Picks isn't designed for dividend income, and many picks pay small or no dividends. Seeking Alpha Premium, with its Dividend Scorecard and ETF Grades, is the better fit for income investors.

My bottom line

Alpha Picks is one of the more transparent stock-picking services out there: published rules, published results, and a clear sell discipline. That's more than most can say. But it is a growth product with a growth product's risks, and the fee only makes sense if it keeps beating a cheap index fund by a healthy margin.

If you're an income investor, start with Premium and your own ETF research instead. If you want a slice of rules-based growth picks on top of a solid index core, Alpha Picks is worth a serious look. Either way, this isn't a buy or sell signal, so do your own due diligence. For more on how ETFs work before you go stock picking, our ETF 101 hub and the top 100 dividend ETFs are good next stops.
